The gap

ECG machines are everywhere. Echo is not.

Echocardiography is the reference standard for assessing how well the heart pumps, but it needs an ultrasound machine and a trained operator — both scarce outside large hospitals. An ECG and a phone are almost everywhere. This is a triage layer: it helps decide who should get the scarce echo slot first.

Step 01

Photograph the strip

Any phone camera, any printed 12-lead ECG. No new hardware, no app to install, no change to how the clinic already works.

Step 02

Send it on WhatsApp

An interface health workers already use all day. Nothing to learn and nothing to log into.

Step 03

Act on the reply

A structured assessment comes back in about a minute, with a referral recommendation the clinician can act on or overrule.

The reply

What comes back.

Written for the person holding the phone, not for a data scientist.

  • How well the heart is pumpingAn estimate of pumping strength, expressed the way a referring clinician would read it.
  • Wall-motion assessmentWhether parts of the heart muscle appear to be moving abnormally.
  • A confidence indicatorHow strongly the system holds the result, so a borderline case is visibly borderline.
  • A referral recommendationMeasured against a threshold the supervising clinician sets for their own site and referral capacity.
  • A heat-mapWhich parts of the trace drove the result, so the clinician can sanity-check it rather than take it on trust.
  • In three languagesEnglish, Kannada and Hindi, chosen to match the health worker rather than the hospital.
A screening aid, not a diagnosis. This is research-stage decision support. It is a first-pass filter that helps prioritise who gets an echocardiogram sooner — it does not replace one, and a normal result does not exclude disease. It is not a regulated medical device and carries no regulatory clearance.

Read these honestly. The severe-EF figure rests on 46 severely reduced cases, not a handful — that is what makes it worth quoting. But at the current threshold the tool catches roughly six in ten patients with a significant wall-motion abnormality, and the data comes from a single cardiology centre with no external or prospective validation yet. Full methodology and the complete results report are available to clinical partners on request.
Where it stands

Running, and looking for partners.

The system is deployed and in use with clinicians in three languages. Referral thresholds are adjustable per site rather than fixed by us, so a centre can tune caution to its own capacity. We are now looking for cardiology centres willing to collaborate on multi-centre data collection and independent validation — the step that matters most for taking this beyond research.
